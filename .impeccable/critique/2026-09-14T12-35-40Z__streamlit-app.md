## Design Specificity Verdict

LLM assessment (A): Partially authored, not fully earned. The Challenge/Did/Found tri-color pattern, the disclaimer, and the citation footer are real project-specific authorship. But CLASS_COLORS (lib/ui.py:3-8), the one brand commitment PRODUCT.md names explicitly, is referenced nowhere else in the codebase.

Deterministic scan (B): The detector's side-tab rule (colored left-border accent + rounded corner) fired 5 times across lib/ui.py (lines 100, 109, 209, 216, 223), and those same declarations reappeared as live DOM findings on both rendered pages - the disclaimer, the key-finding callout, and all three narrative cards use the visually identical treatment.

Visual overlays: not available in this harness (no browser-automation tool); B's URL-based Puppeteer scans served as the browser-rendered evidence instead.

## Overall Impression

The narrative skeleton is well-judged for a jury audience and proven on one real page, but that page is currently the weakest test of the product's own principles: hardcoded numbers instead of reports/ JSON sourcing, and an inert class-color system. B's browser measurements also surfaced a severe, previously invisible defect: several strings, including "Berfin & Mert" on the cover, render at 1.0-1.2:1 contrast - functionally unreadable, not merely "borderline."

## What's Working

1. The Challenge/Did/Found pattern (ui.py:191-226) turns dense EDA methodology into a legible 3-beat story, already proven on a real page.
2. Sequencing for a jury: hero, disclaimer, metrics, roadmap is a well-judged emotional order.
3. Statistical honesty embedded inline - effect sizes sit next to their charts rather than being buried.

## Priority Issues

[P0] Severe low-contrast text on the cover and Page 1
- What: Browser-measured contrast as low as 1.0:1 on "Berfin & Mert" (need 3-4.5:1), 2.7:1 on the main title, 1.1-2.1:1 on eyebrow/context/caption/footer text, on desktop and mobile.
- Why it matters: This is the first thing a juror sees; a baseline legibility failure unrelated to the earlier colorblind-safety decision.
- Fix: Audit every text element on the gradient hero background and every opacity-stacked caption/footer, replace with a fixed color verified at >=4.5:1 (>=3:1 large text).
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

[P1] CLASS_COLORS is fully defined but completely unused
- What: lib/ui.py:3-8 defines 4 class colors; zero other references; Page 1's class tables render plain text.
- Why it matters: PRODUCT.md names this palette an existing brand commitment; pages 3-6 will each need per-class breakdowns.
- Fix: Add a class_badge/class_legend helper to lib/ui.py, retrofit Page 1's three tables.
- Suggested command: /impeccable colorize

[P1] Metrics-sourcing principle (Product Principle #2) is unexercised
- What: All Page 1 numbers are hardcoded Python literals; lib/metrics.py's loaders are dead code; no reports/eda/*.json exists.
- Why it matters: "Numbers read from reports/ JSON, never retyped" is the app's strongest credibility claim, unproven by the one built page.
- Fix: Export EDA stats to reports/eda/summary.json and load through a metrics.py-style helper, or document EDA as a scoped exception.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

[P1] Unguarded st.image() calls risk a live crash in front of the jury
- What: 4 unguarded st.image() calls on Page 1, no existence check or try/except.
- Why it matters: A missing figure throws a raw Streamlit traceback mid-presentation.
- Fix: Wrap in an existence check with a graceful fallback.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

[P2] The strongest EDA evidence is hidden behind unsignaled tabs
- What: The RGB-encoding confound and duplicate-concentration findings sit in tabs 2-3 of 4, defaulting to tab 1.
- Why it matters: The evidence most load-bearing for the project's differentiator is easiest to miss.
- Fix: Add a one-line pointer above the tabs, or reorder so the RGB-encoding finding leads.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify
